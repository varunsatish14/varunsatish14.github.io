My research interests center on understanding the physical processes that shape galaxies and how we can better model them through computational methods.

## Computational Astrophysics

My primary research focuses on galaxy formation and evolution using cosmological simulations. I am particularly interested in stellar feedback, radiative transfer, and stellar population modeling, including how different initial mass functions (IMFs) influence the evolution of simulated galaxies.

My current work involves modifying the hydrodynamical simulation code [ENZO](https://enzo-project.org/) to model stellar populations using different IMFs and investigate their effects on galaxy evolution. I am also exploring stellar population synthesis with [FSPS](https://dfm.io/python-fsps/current/) to better understand stellar modelling.

Previously, I studied the escape of ionizing radiation from galaxies during the Epoch of Reionization, developing numerical radiative transfer methods to calculate escape fractions and investigate how stellar feedback and local gas properties influence the escape of radiation.

I have also contributed to the development and open-source preparation of [**Haskap Pie**](https://github.com/ksbarrow/Haskap_Pie), a halo-finding and analysis tool designed for cosmological simulations. My work has included maintaining our simulation analysis pipeline and using Haskap Pie to serve as the primary code maintainer for our group.

## Astronomy Education

I am also involved in astronomy education research, studying the quality of astronomy education in high schools. This work later expanded into a nationwide study of high school astronomy courses, where we investigated how astronomy education has changed across all fifty states.

I have also collaborated with Professor Edward Prather at the University of Arizona on the development of an undergraduate research skills bootcamp. As the only student member of the working group, I contribute a student perspective to the development of active-learning activities and research-centered educational experiences. This work has reinforced my belief that students learn science most effectively when they have opportunities to investigate questions themselves.
